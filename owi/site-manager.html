What that means

Turnover has not cost OWI a cent more on our invoice. We bill only hours worked, so a no-show, a day-one walk-off or an empty seat bills $0. The recruiting, screening and onboarding behind 127 starts in 90 days is all inside a markup that went down.

What the lower markup can't pay for. Today Bob Petrisko and Shauna Whipple spend two full days every week on site at OWI: greeting starts, walking the floor, sorting out problems with your supervisors. At 1.35 we can't afford that time, let alone more of it. A dedicated site manager has to be funded, and OWI is already holding the money to fund it: $2,250 a week in savings from the rate cut.

Who owns retention

We deliver the people. Most are lost after they reach your floor.

115 assignments ended between 14 August and 12 November. Here is how they ended, when, and what the people who left told us.

How the ends happened

Almost half were not people leaving. They were ended under OWI's attendance-point, performance and conduct rules, at a median of 14 days. People who walk away go at a median of 2.5 days.

When the ends happened

Only 28 (24%) happened on day zero or one, the screening and show-up window we own. The other 87 (76%) happened after the person started on OWI's floor.

Where responsibility sits, in the research

No published study puts a percentage on how much of temp-worker retention belongs to the agency and how much to the host. What the research does is divide it by who controls what, and that points to the host site.

  1. Support from the client company is linked to temp workers' commitment to the client and to their agency. Connelly, Gallagher & Gilley, Journal of Vocational Behavior, 2007; building on Coyle-Shapiro & Morrow, 2006.
  2. Contingent workers commit separately to the agency and to the client company, and how the client treats them shapes their commitment to the client. Liden, Wayne, Kraimer & Sparrowe, Journal of Organizational Behavior, 2003.
  3. "The staffing agency and the staffing agency's client (the host employer) are joint employers of temporary workers." The host provides site-specific training and day-to-day supervision. OSHA & NIOSH, Protecting Temporary Workers.
  4. Managers account for at least 70% of the variance in employee engagement scores. Gallup.
  5. Hourly manufacturing and logistics workers rank "treat me with respect" as the top thing that keeps them loyal, ahead of training and schedule. Employbridge, Voice of the American Workforce 2023, 29,000+ hourly workers.

How the model works

Today 127 starts produce 36 people still working after a quarter (28%). To keep the same 36 at a higher keep rate takes fewer starts: at 40%, it takes 90. The difference is replacements OWI no longer has to train, multiplied by what each one costs. OWI's own replacement cost will be more accurate than ours; every figure scales with it.

The proposal

From two days a week to every shift.

Bob Petrisko and Shauna Whipple are already at OWI two full days a week. A full-time site manager makes that presence permanent, on both shifts, and does three jobs at once: recruits on site, keeps the people you have, and teaches every new associate how OWI works.

Recruiting

  • Hiring events, on-site interviews and plant tours, so candidates see the job first
  • On-site interviews for direct-hire positions
  • Works the rehire list: 13 of 19 leavers would come back
  • Keeps a full bench, about 10 people ahead every week, to cover churn and call-offs the same shift
  • Spots temps worth converting to OWI

Retention

  • A constant presence in the plant: confirms every worker every day and walks the floor
  • Confirms every start the night before, meets them at the door
  • Day-three and day-ten check-ins, where most people leave
  • Calls every no-show within 15 minutes of shift start
  • One partner for every supervisor, on both shifts
  • Exit and stay interviews, a real reason for every end

Educating workers

  • Explains OWI's point policy at orientation and shares balances weekly
  • Warns before the last point, not after
  • Honest job previews: pace, heat, fumes, physical work
  • Safety awareness, safe lifting and forklift training where needed
  • Safety walks and PPE checks with OWI safety

Why being on site works

Ready access to people. Greets every start on 1st and 2nd shift and is there when someone is about to walk.

Reads on people. Talks with associates throughout the day and spots trouble before it becomes a no-show or a quit.

Time to do onboarding right. Plant tours, a proper orientation, the attendance rules explained, PPE and the safety walk, instead of a rushed handoff.

Walks the floor every day. The same floor visits that cover every worker at PCNA, so nobody goes a week without being checked on.
